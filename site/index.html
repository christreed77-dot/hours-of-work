Average U.S. price in dollars, not adjusted for inflation.

How this is calculated

  • Hours of work = price of one unit ÷ hourly wage. Both are in the dollars of the day, so inflation cancels out and nothing is adjusted.
  • Wages come from the BLS Current Population Survey: usual weekly earnings of full-time wage and salary workers, converted to hourly by dividing by 40 hours. Each month uses the wage for its quarter; when the newest quarter isn't published yet, the latest available one is used.
  • Wage levels are the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 90th percentiles of usual weekly earnings, published directly by BLS. A person at the 25th percentile earns more than 25% of full-time workers.
  • Prices come from different sources and update at different speeds. Some are measured directly; some are estimates built from a price index and one published dollar figure. Every item's notice (under the summary tiles) says which, and the table below lists every series.
  • This is a pre-tax measure and covers full-time workers only. It shows how long a person at that wage level works to buy one unit, not what a household spends.
